# Language

We write like a student who builds things and is telling a friend where to be tonight: short sentences, real numbers, no hype.

## Voice

| We are | So we write | Not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plain | "Doors at 6, build until midnight, demo to judges." | "An electrifying overnight innovation experience." |
| Specific | "100 to 150 students", "Around $500 in credits or prizes" | "Hundreds of top students", "Generous perks" |
| Honest | "One confirmed night and a year of placeholders." | Dates we have not booked shown as if they were real |
| Dry | "Seven students. Zero sleep on the 14th." | Exclamation marks, emoji |
| Direct | "Come alone and we run team formation at 7 PM." | "Participants are encouraged to…" |

- Address the reader as **you**; we are **we**. Students and sponsors are both "you" in their own sections.
- Say why in the same breath: "Most projects die in the planning stage, so a one-night build removes the planning stage."
- One idea per sentence. If a sentence needs a semicolon, try two sentences first.
- No exclamation marks, no emoji, no "excited to announce", no "innovative", "cutting-edge", "world-class", "passionate", "leverage", "synergy".

## Casing and punctuation

- Headlines and buttons: sentence case ("Sponsor the night", "Get notified").
- Mono labels: uppercase via CSS, written in sentence case in the source ("Seats, Nov 14").
- Section labels: "01 — About". Two-digit number, spaced em dash, name.
- The middle dot ( · ) separates facts on one line: "Student-run · UC Berkeley · Est. 2026".
- Brackets mark a slot to fill: "Best use of [Track Sponsor A]". Pair it with a `Badge`.
- Ranges in sentences are words: "100 to 150". A timeline span uses an arrow: "2026 → 2027".

## Numbers, dates and times

- Numerals always, even under ten, when they are a count or a time: "3 minutes per team", "Teams are usually two to four people" (words are fine for a loose range in a sentence).
- Times: 12-hour, no leading zero, uppercase AM/PM with a space: "6:00 PM", "12:15 AM". Drop ":00" in prose ("Doors 6 PM"), keep it in schedules and labels.
- Time zone once per view, where it matters: "6:00 PM PST".
- Dates: "Saturday, November 14, 2026" in full; "Sat, Nov 14" in labels and tickers; "January 2027" when only the month is known.
- Money: "$0", "Around $500 in credits or prizes". Never "$500+" or "up to".
- Editions and sections use two digits: 01, 02, S1.

## Buttons and links

- Verb first, two or three words: "Sponsor us", "Get notified", "Propose an event", "Back to top", "Copy address".
- Say what happens, then confirm in the same words: "Copy address" → "Copied".
- Every button and link ends with the arrow glyph; never "Click here" or "Learn more".

## Placeholders

- Anything not confirmed says so in the copy and with a `Badge`: "Date and room to be confirmed.", "Form link TBD", "Follow URL TBD".
- Never invent a sponsor, a venue, a judge or a quote to fill a layout. Use a bracketed slot.
- Email until the inbox exists: sponsors@foundersquare.example, marked "Replace before launch".

## Examples from the site

- Hero: "A student-run community of builders and founders at UC Berkeley. Our first event is Build Night: one night, one project, one demo, on Saturday, November 14."
- Section lead: "A room for people who build."
- Sponsors: "Build Night puts 100 to 150 Berkeley students in a room for one night, hands them your product, and asks them to ship something with it before midnight."
- FAQ: "Do I need an idea?" "No. We post starter ideas at kickoff and sponsors bring problems they want solved."
- Closing: "Let's build something at 1 AM."
- Footer: "Student-run, not a university program."
